**Tool:** Power BI  
**Author:** Mohammad Ali  
**Phase:** Power BI – Business Analysis  

---

## 🎯 Objective

The objective of Day 48 was to analyse **operational workload and order behaviour** using transactional data.

Instead of focusing on revenue alone, this analysis shifts attention to **order volume, distribution, and operational pressure**, helping identify where effort is concentrated across regions, customers, warehouses, and time.

---

## 🧠 Why This Analysis Matters

Revenue indicates how big a business is.  
Orders indicate how much **work** the business is doing.

Operational inefficiencies often remain hidden when analysis focuses only on sales and profit.  
Order behaviour provides valuable insight into:
- Fulfilment pressure
- Capacity planning
- Workload imbalance
- Cost-to-serve risks

---

## 🔍 What Was Analysed

- Total orders by region  
- Customer-level order concentration  
- Warehouse-wise order distribution  
- Monthly order trends over time  
- Order behaviour as a proxy for operational effort  

---

## 📊 Key Insights

- Order volume is unevenly distributed across regions, creating concentrated operational load  
- A small set of customers generates a disproportionately high number of orders  
- Operational dependency exists on a limited number of warehouses  
- Monthly order trends show fluctuations that impact planning and resource allocation  
- High operational effort does not always align with high business value  

---

## 💼 Business Impact & Recommendations

- Track operational load alongside revenue and profit metrics  
- Review high-order customers for efficiency and cost-to-serve optimisation  
- Reduce dependency on single warehouses to lower operational risk  
- Plan staffing and resources around high-order volume periods  
- Use order behaviour metrics to support operational decision-making  

---

## 🎯 Key Learning

Revenue shows growth.  
Orders show effort.

Understanding order behaviour is essential for building **scalable, efficient, and resilient operations**.
